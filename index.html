Business case study · Aditya Birla Fashion & Retail

OWND! Rollout Decision

Pantaloons lost 0.8 points of EBITDA margin in FY26 as ABFRL scaled its new value format, OWND!. I built the case for what to do next, using only public data.

Question
Should ABFRL accelerate, hold or slow the OWND! rollout? I split it into three tests: is OWND! competing with Zudio, is it taking Pantaloons' customers, and is its drag on profit shrinking?
Data & method
13 quarters of investor presentations, about 67,000 products from the OWND! and Pantaloons catalogues, and 1,476 stores from three store locators. I compared price bands, measured pincode co-location against fair baselines, and estimated OWND!'s unreported revenue from two equations. One Python script rebuilds every table and chart.
Findings
OWND! matches Zudio on price, with half its best-sellers at ₹500 or less. 83% of its products in shared categories sit below Pantaloons' best-seller price band, so the overlap is concentrated in T-shirts. But the margin drag has not shrunk in four quarters and has cost about ₹38 cr of EBITDA.
Recommendation
A gated hold: up to 20 stores in FY28, split between metro in-fill and a Tier 2 test, with about 40 more released only when OWND!'s own store economics turn positive. If OWND! runs at a −5% margin, accelerating instead would put about ₹23 cr a year more at risk.

Credit risk model · Live Streamlit app

Company Bankruptcy Scorecard

A credit score that predicts whether a company will go bankrupt within a year, and shows which financial ratios pulled its score down.

Question
Can a scorecard that a bank can audit, using only a handful of ratios, come close to a black-box model?
Data & method
5,850 Polish companies with 64 financial ratios each. I binned each ratio by Weight of Evidence so risk changes steadily from bin to bin, kept ratios by Information Value, correlation and cross-validated AUC, and fitted a logistic regression scaled to points: 600 points means 50-to-1 odds of staying solvent, and every 20 points doubles them. LightGBM on all 64 ratios is the benchmark, with bootstrap confidence intervals on both.
Result
Test AUC of 0.892 with 6 ratios, against 0.951 for LightGBM with 64. The scorecard gives up 0.059 AUC in exchange for a score anyone can trace back to its inputs, which is what lenders need to explain and audit decisions.

IEEE conference paper · InC4 2026

Deep JSCC for Robust Image Transmission over Multipath OFDM Channels

Neural radio transmitters usually need a channel model they can backpropagate through, which real hardware doesn't provide. This paper trains the transmitter with reinforcement learning instead.

Problem
Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding sends images over noisy wireless links, but existing models only train over channels written as differentiable maths. That rules out real, black-box OFDM systems with multipath fading.
Approach
The CNN encoder acts as a reinforcement-learning agent, updated with REINFORCE policy gradients over a black-box OFDM simulator (64 subcarriers, 8-tap Rayleigh fading). A two-stage coarse-to-fine residual receiver rebuilds the image. Training runs in three phases: AWGN pre-training, RL over OFDM, then SSIM fine-tuning.
Result
29.05 dB PSNR (0.91 SSIM) on SVHN and 22.17 dB (0.73 SSIM) on CIFAR-10. That is up to 17 dB and 10.6 dB better than a baseline trained on a simple noise channel, which fails outright on multipath fading. Images stay recognisable even at 0 dB SNR.
